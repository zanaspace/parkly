Parking Marketplace
Market and Competitive Research for Product Strategy
Prepared 7 October 2026 (updated with follow-up research). Launch market assumed: Lagos, Nigeria, with global benchmarks.
1. Executive summary
The idea is a product connecting car owners to parking lot spaces. This report maps the global and African landscape, the Lagos operating context, and the gaps a new entrant could use.
Demand is real, rails are thin. Lagos has an estimated 25M+ residents and 3M+ vehicles, and the state parking authority (LASPA) states that parking demand exceeds supply. African competitors are small; the one scaled player, Admyt in South Africa, sells to property owners rather than to drivers.
Global consolidation has happened. Uber closed its acquisition of SpotHero on 16 April 2026. EasyPark Group has added ParkMobile, Flowbird and Parkopedia. Metropolis bought SP+ for $1.5B. Incumbents are bundled into larger platforms, which leaves emerging markets open but raises the odds of a well-funded entrant later.
Trust and operations decide winners. Parkwell (Lagos) reports that earning user trust has been hard. KaChing (South Africa) shut down in 2025 after app reliability and support problems while Admyt scaled.
Government will own the street. Lagos plans paid, digital on-street parking collected into government accounts. A private product should focus on off-street lots, monthly parking, venues and events.
Recommended wedge (judgment). Start with commercial lots and monthly parking in one or two dense nodes, paired with operator software and a visible trust layer. Delay peer-to-peer driveway sharing.
2. Scope, method and confidence
Findings come from public web sources: company and press releases, regulatory filings, trade and local press, and aggregator profiles. No primary research (driver or operator interviews) has been done yet.
Higher confidence: acquisitions and scale claims from filings and press releases (Uber/SpotHero, EasyPark Group), LASPA statements.
Lower confidence: market-size forecasts from paywalled aggregators, startup traction figures from 2023, and pricing quoted in 2022 government statements.
Not verified: see Section 10 for follow-up research on the original open questions, and Section 11 for what remains unknown.
3. Market size
Published figures disagree because each report defines the market differently. Examples found: parking finder apps at about $2.8B in 2026; mobile parking apps at $3.53B in 2025; smart parking systems at $12.88B in 2025 (a broader category including hardware and software); parking management at $4.45B in 2026. Use these only to show the category is growing, not as a TAM.
Recommended bottom-up approach for Lagos:
Vehicles in target zones (start from LASPA’s 3M+ vehicle figure and narrow to commercial nodes).
Share of drivers who regularly pay for off-street or monthly parking.
Average spend per driver per month (needs fresh price checks; a 2022 government statement cited N500–N1,000 per hour at private commercial parks).
Achievable take rate or software subscription per lot.
Supply signal: LASPA says it is lane-marking about 1,800 additional on-street slots across Ikeja, Surulere, Victoria Island, Ikoyi and Lekki, which is small against 3M+ vehicles.

4.2 Business model patterns
Commission on bookings (SpotHero, ParkWhiz): aligned with successful transactions; volume partners negotiate lower rates.
Two-sided fees (JustPark charges both driver and space owner), plus revenue-share with hotels at no upfront cost.
Software sold to property owners (Admyt): landlords pay for equipment and tools; drivers use the app free.
Data and licensing (Parkopedia): coverage claims of 99% in Europe sold to automakers and others.
Government payments (Nairobi, planned Lagos): state collects directly through its own payment system.
5. Lagos context
Scale and shortage: about 25M residents and 3M+ vehicles; LASPA says demand exceeds supply.
On-street reform: announced for October 2024, then repeatedly delayed. Latest reporting describes a pilot in Ikeja (200 roads listed) planned around July 2026 or before end of 2026, paid through digital channels into government accounts, with clamping and towing for violations.
Registration and levies: LASPA expects private and commercial parking locations to register, and charges levies on setback parking (N80,000 per slot per year in a 2022 notice). Lot owners carry compliance obligations a product could simplify.
Informal fees: Lagos authorities have warned motorists against paying touts for unauthorised parking.
Security: US consular security guidance advises parking in guarded or access-controlled areas because of vehicle theft risk.
Pricing signal (dated): N500–N1,000 per hour at private commercial parks (2022 government statement); airport multi-level car park opened 2017 with 1,300 capacity. Refresh before using in a model.
Regional analog: Nairobi moved parking to cashless county payments. Reported issues included user confusion and an outage that briefly meant free parking.

8. Risks
Supply quality: unreliable lots or attendants destroy trust quickly (the KaChing lesson).
Regulatory change: LASPA scope may widen; align early and treat registration as a feature.
Platform entrants: Uber now owns SpotHero, and ride-hailing apps could add parking in new markets.
Payments and connectivity: outages (Nairobi) and cash habits can undercut digital adoption.
Low willingness to pay: if informal parking stays cheap and tolerated, premium trust features may not convert.

10. Follow-up research (7 October 2026)
This section answers the five open questions from the first version of the report.
10.1 LASPA on-street pilot
No report found that the on-street pilot has started. Coverage from around May 2026 says it will begin within 2026 in selected areas including Ikeja.
The scheme is described as fully cashless and pay-by-plate, with payments going directly into government accounts.
The most recent LASPA news found (1 September 2026) is enforcement of 2026 parking permits for existing registered customers, a separate programme from on-street parking.
No payment partner is named for on-street. ARCA Payment Limited was announced in 2023 to develop a parking payment application, but it is unconfirmed whether this is the same system. Action: ask LASPA directly.
10.2 Parkwell: current status
Appears active, now trading as JustParkwell (Parkwell Mobile Services Ltd). Play Store listing updated 23 October 2025; founder activity shows a Senegal version under development and fleet-parking messaging for Nigeria.
Last published traction is from 2023: 3,150 users (Techpoint) and 5,000+ users (BusinessDay), 80 lots, 2,000+ spaces. No newer numbers found.
Business model: commission of up to 30% of the parking fee.
Funding: $20K seed from an accelerator (January 2023) per CB Insights. One directory shows $750K, which appears to be accelerator cohort benefits rather than a Parkwell round; treat as unreliable.

10.5 Platform and fintech entry into Nigerian parking
Uber: the SpotHero integration covers US and Canadian inventory (13,000+ locations in 400+ cities). Nothing found about Nigeria.
Bolt: Nigeria’s most downloaded mobility app; no parking announcements found. Bolt ended Bolt Food in Nigeria and South Africa in December 2023, suggesting it prunes non-core products.
Banks and fintechs (Moniepoint, OPay, Paga, Interswitch): no parking products found, but they control payment rails.
Assessment: no announced threat. Absence of evidence is not proof. If platforms do enter, they would likely partner for supply, which makes a LASPA-compliant operator network a possible integration partner (inference).
10.6 What changes in the strategy
Stay focused on off-street while on-street remains unlaunched and state-controlled.
Treat LASPA registration checks and payment structure as launch gates, not later features.
Do not plan around a platform entrant in the next year, but monitor.
