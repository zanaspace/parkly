2. Strategic insights driving the strategy
Demand exceeds supply, and the state is formalising the street. Lagos has 3M+ vehicles by LASPA’s estimate. Paid on-street parking is planned, cashless and routed to government accounts. That leaves off-street inventory as the private opportunity.
Trust is the unsolved problem. The closest local competitor reports trust as its hardest hurdle. Informal fees and theft risk shape driver behaviour. One legal commentary reads Nigerian case law as leaving risk with the driver when the driver keeps the keys, so a claims promise could be a real differentiator (confirm with counsel).
Software for operators scales faster than apps for drivers. Admyt reached 92 locations and 150,000+ bays in South Africa by serving landlords, while a driver-first app, KaChing, shut down over reliability and support.
Compliance is a moat, not just a cost. LASPA requires all public-facing parking operators to register and began enforcing 2026 permits in September. A product that checks permits at listing time protects drivers and keeps the platform clean.
Global incumbents are not in this market yet. Uber’s SpotHero deal covers the US and Canada. No Nigeria parking launch from Uber, Bolt or the major fintechs was found, but the gap will not last forever.

5. Strategic pillars
Pillar 1: Verified trust layer
Lot verification checklist: LASPA permit, guard presence, lighting, CCTV, access control, capacity count, owner identity.
Ratings and incident reporting after every session; fast removal of poor lots.
Claims process for damage or theft, backed by an insurance partner once cost and rules are validated.
Pillar 2: Operator operating system
Inventory and occupancy tracking, simple check-in (QR or plate entry), attendant app that works on a low-end phone.
Cashless collection with daily settlement and reports; permit and levy reminders.
Free basic tools; paid tier for analytics, staff controls and integrations.
Pillar 3: Cashless certainty
Price shown before booking, extend or exit in app, digital receipt.
Payments through a licensed gateway with split settlement; do not hold customer balances.
Resilient to poor connectivity and power: offline-tolerant attendant flow, SMS fallback.
Pillar 4: Compliance by design
A valid LASPA permit is a listing requirement; permit status is visible to the team.
Data protection basics from day one: consent, minimal data, retention rules; assess NDPC registration.
Legal opinion on payments structure, liability terms and insurance before public launch.

8. Go-to-market
Principles
Supply first. Drivers will not use an app with few reliable lots. Sign and verify lots before spending on driver marketing.
One node. Pick one district for density. Choose by survey: Victoria Island/Ikoyi/Lekki for willingness to pay, Ikeja for commercial density and the planned on-street pilot.
Concierge before software. Run the first lots manually with a booking form, payment link and WhatsApp support to learn the operations.
Sequence
Sign 10–15 lots (hotels, office buildings, commercial lots) with a free pilot and a clear revenue share.
Verify each lot and publish verified status; record baseline occupancy.
Acquire commuters through employers, building managers and office-park networks, then referrals.
Add event centres and churches for peak demand once operations are stable.
Open a LASPA conversation early: compliance check, pilot alignment, no surprises.
Channels
Direct sales to lot owners and facility managers; employer partnerships; WhatsApp and social groups for commuters; on-site signage at partner lots; referral credits.

11. Product and operations requirements
Platforms: Android-first driver app and lightweight web; simple attendant app or PWA for operators.
Core flows: search by destination, availability, booking, pay, entry check-in, extend, exit, receipt, rating, claim.
Payments: licensed gateway, split settlement to operators, reconciliation, refund and dispute handling.
Identity and safety: verified phone, vehicle plate on account, lot verification records, incident log.
Resilience: offline-tolerant check-in, SMS notifications, power-aware operations at lots.
Support: human support from day one with a fast response target; a claims owner.
Team (pilot): product lead, 2–3 engineers, operations lead for lot onboarding and verification, support agents, part-time legal and compliance adviser.

14. Key assumptions to validate
Commuters will pay for guaranteed, safe parking and will book ahead.
Lot owners will accept a 10–15% share and adopt cashless collection.
Enough verified lots exist in one node to offer a real choice.
A claims promise is affordable and insurable.
LASPA will welcome compliant private platforms rather than treat them as competitors.
